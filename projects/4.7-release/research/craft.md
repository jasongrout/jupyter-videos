## 2. Pacing and retention

Wistia (13 M videos, 2025) reports 52 % average engagement for videos under 1 minute, and over 50 % for 1 to 5 minute tutorials. It recommends under 1 minute for social and 1 to 5 minutes for explainers ([Wistia](https://wistia.com/learn/marketing/optimal-video-length)). YouTube's "Intro" metric is the share of viewers still watching at 30 s. A strong value means the first 30 s matched the title and thumbnail ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en)). Facebook says "The first few seconds of every video are critical" and suggests cutting long videos to 3 minutes ([Facebook](https://creators.facebook.com/blog/10-video-best-practices)). A Facebook-commissioned report asks for movement at the start, the message and branding up front, and a video that works with sound off ([SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/facebooks-11-tips-for-better-video-ads/416559/)). No primary source measures a 3 s against a 10 s hook; the "3 seconds" figures in search results come from vendor blogs.

Seconds per feature, from chapters: VS Code 11 to 26 s, Warp 10 to 33 s, Figma about 13 s, Blender 5.1 about 59 s per area ([Blender 5.1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8r3xoRwwN6I)).

Social limits and autoplay:

- X allows 2:20 for non-paying accounts ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/10/bluesky-now-lets-users-upload-videos-that-are-up-to-3-minutes-long/)). X's own help page refused automated reads, so this is secondary.
- LinkedIn ads recommend 15 to 30 s, loop videos under 30 s, recommend 4:5, take SRT captions and require under 30 fps ([LinkedIn ads](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/85306)). Organic posts take 3 s to 15 min at 10 to 60 fps ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a548372)).
- Bluesky allows 3 minutes since March 2025 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/10/bluesky-now-lets-users-upload-videos-that-are-up-to-3-minutes-long/)). Videos autoplay by default and can carry subtitles ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/09/11/bluesky-catches-up-to-x-with-native-support-for-video)).
- Mastodon disables GIF autoplay by default ([docs](https://docs.joinmastodon.org/user/preferences/)), and a silent loop is a "gifv" ([docs](https://docs.joinmastodon.org/entities/MediaAttachment/)). mastodon.social accepts up to 99 MB, 120 fps and 3840 x 2160 pixels ([API](https://mastodon.social/api/v2/instance)).
- YouTube Shorts take up to 3 minutes for square or taller video since 15 October 2024 ([YouTube](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/tall-updates-coming-to-shorts/)).

Buffer found square video gave "30-35% higher video views" in some cases, but landscape won on desktop ([Buffer 2017](https://buffer.com/resources/square-video-vs-landscape-video/)). Vertical beat square on Instagram ([Buffer 2019](https://buffer.com/resources/vertical-video)). Both are paid tests on one audience, so the evidence is weak.

## 3. Screen-recording polish

Cap (open source) gives the clearest numbers. Its default auto zoom is 2.0x. A zoom starts 300 ms before a click and holds 2.5 s after it. Zooms closer than 2.5 s merge, and clicks in the last 1 s are ignored ([auto_zoom.rs](https://github.com/CapSoftware/Cap/blob/4fa468edc9056b1907ed1bb580852be6dfcd418e/crates/project/src/auto_zoom.rs#L9-L18)). Cap replaced duration easing with a spring. New projects use stiffness 205, damping 33.3 and mass 1.5, which "reaches 90% of a zoom in 317 ms with no overshoot and settles within 1% by 500 ms" ([configuration.rs](https://github.com/CapSoftware/Cap/blob/4fa468edc9056b1907ed1bb580852be6dfcd418e/crates/project/src/configuration.rs#L1065-L1074)). The click ripple is off by default, lasts 0.6 s and accepts 0.2 to 1.5 s ([configuration.rs](https://github.com/CapSoftware/Cap/blob/4fa468edc9056b1907ed1bb580852be6dfcd418e/crates/project/src/configuration.rs#L974-L980)). New projects get even padding, a softer shadow and corners of "about 30 px on a 1080p screen" ([configuration.rs](https://github.com/CapSoftware/Cap/blob/4fa468edc9056b1907ed1bb580852be6dfcd418e/crates/project/src/configuration.rs#L3309-L3311)).

Screen Studio's "Focused" animation "stabilizes quickly, making it easier to follow and read the content". Its "Smooth" option is for content "not focused on reading". Motion blur is set apart for the cursor, the zoom and the pan ([Screen Studio](https://screen.studio/guide/animations)). The cursor can grow, hide when idle, drop shakes and return to its start for a loop ([cursor](https://screen.studio/guide/cursor)). The app shows detected shortcuts, shows single keys only on request, filters out typing and has a label size slider ([shortcuts](https://screen.studio/guide/shortcuts)). Tella, FocuSee and Rotato documentation gave no numbers, so their defaults stay open.

Motion sickness: WebKit lists zooming (an illusion of moving forward), spinning, parallax, 2.5D plane shifts and horizontal motion at the edge of vision as triggers ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/7551/responsive-design-for-motion/)). Apple's criteria add animated blur and depth of field ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-accessibility/reduced-motion-evaluation-criteria)). A video cannot follow the viewer's Reduce Motion setting, so the edit must keep these effects small.

Frame rate: Screen Studio says 60 fps looks "smoother and more polished" and 24 or 30 fps is standard ([export](https://screen.studio/guide/explanation-of-export-settings)). For Plasma 6.5 a user said the clips made KDE look slow and asked for a higher frame rate, and KDE remade three clips ([KDE Discuss](https://discuss.kde.org/t/plasma-6-5-is-out-look-forward-to-lots-cool-stuff/40796)).

Legibility, by my arithmetic: JupyterLab UI text is 13 px and content text 14 px (`packages/theme-light-extension/style/variables.css`). A portrait phone shows a 1920 px frame about 390 px wide (assumed phone width), a 0.2 scale, so 13 px becomes 2.6 px. Recording at device scale factor 2 with a 2x zoom gives 52 px in the frame: 10 px in portrait and 23 px in full-screen landscape.

## 4. On-screen text

A meta-analysis of 190 studies puts average silent reading at 238 words per minute ([BPS](https://www.bps.org.uk/research-digest/most-comprehensive-review-date-finds-average-persons-reading-speed-slower)). Ofcom caps subtitles on pre-recorded programmes at 160 to 180 words per minute ([Ofcom](https://ofcom.org.uk/tv-radio-and-on-demand/accessibility/access-services-review)). Netflix allows 20 characters per second for adults, 42 characters per line and 2 lines ([Netflix](https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/217350977-English-USA-Timed-Text-Style-Guide)), with a minimum of 20 frames and a 2 frame gap ([timing](https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/360051554394-Timed-Text-Style-Guide-Subtitle-Timing-Guidelines)). At 160 words per minute a 6 word headline needs 2.25 s.

Burned-in text does not reach automatic translation, so translated subtitle files are needed ([Rev](https://rev.com/blog/benefits-of-burn-in-captions), a vendor; weak). LinkedIn and Bluesky accept caption files.

WCAG 2.3.1 allows no more than three flashes in any one second, or flashes below the area and luminance thresholds. A general flash is a pair of opposite changes of 10 % relative luminance, and video is checked at full screen ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html)). WCAG 1.4.3 asks for 4.5:1 contrast, or 3:1 for text of 18 pt or 14 pt bold; the page does not mention video, so it applies by analogy ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)). WCAG 1.2.1 asks for a text alternative or an audio track for video-only content, and W3C leaves open whether background music makes a video "video-only" ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/audio-only-and-video-only-prerecorded.html)).

## 5. Narration, music or text only

Descript praises a film without voiceover because it works muted, and a narrated film for its "soothing voiceover backed by a strong script" ([Descript](https://descript.com/blog/article/product-launch-videos)). Facebook autoplays without sound ([SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/facebooks-11-tips-for-better-video-ads/416559/)). Whether developer audiences accept synthetic voices is open: no source was read.

Music licences:

- YouTube Audio Library: standard tracks need no credit, Creative Commons tracks need credit in the description, and downloads "won't be claimed by a rights holder through the Content ID system" ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/3376882?hl=en)). The page does not say whether the tracks may be used on X, LinkedIn, Bluesky or Mastodon, so that stays open.
- Pixabay: no credit is needed and standalone resale is banned. The summary does not mention Content ID ([Pixabay](https://pixabay.com/service/license-summary/)), so claims stay open.
- Incompetech: the free licence "Requires that you credit the music", and the site generates the credit text ([Incompetech](https://incompetech.com/music/royalty-free/licenses/)).
- Creative Commons 4.0: music "synched in timed relation with a moving image" is always Adapted Material, so NoDerivatives tracks cannot go in a video ([CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/legalcode.en)).
- Free Music Archive licences vary per track and were not read.

Loudness is open: no primary source was read. The common figure of -14 LUFS for YouTube is a practitioner figure.

## 6. Open-source practice

- Blender's official overviews are presented by an outside education company with guest creators per area: 43:35 for 4.5 LTS and 13:44 for 5.1, each in 14 chapters. Descriptions thank "developers, users reporting issues, content creators making documentation, donators and members of the Development Fund" and link the notes and the fund ([4.5](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wPhA0imjvVs)). The 1:40 showcase reel credits about 25 artists.
- GNOME posts a 30 to 64 s film per release. The same description cites "countless hours of work from hundreds of contributors" and links notes, get involved and donate. The release post links the film ([Discourse](https://discourse.gnome.org/t/introducing-gnome-47/23399)). Who makes the films is not stated.
- KDE embeds short per-feature MP4 and WebM clips in the announcement, which ends with get involved, donate and a live image ([KDE](https://kde.org/announcements/plasma/6/6.4.0/)).
- VS Code: monthly highlights by a team member, plus a 1:08:00 "Release Recap" stream. Zed: 1.0 film and 4 to 7 minute "Introducing" films.
- For Inkscape, Obsidian and Excalidraw a YouTube search found only third-party videos.

## Rules for our video

1. The main cut runs 90 to 150 s and shows 5 to 7 features (VS Code 2:51 with 7, Warp 2:27 with 6, Wistia 1 to 5 min).
2. Each feature gets 15 to 25 s in the main cut (VS Code 11 to 26 s, Warp 10 to 33 s).
3. The first feature is on screen by 0:05; the title card lasts 3 s or less, with no logo animation first (Facebook, SEJ).
4. The strongest feature plays inside the first 30 s, because YouTube's Intro metric is measured at 0:30.
5. YouTube chapters mark every feature, as VS Code and Warp do.
6. Every feature also exists as its own 15 to 30 s clip (LinkedIn ads 15 to 30 s; Linear Releases 0:30).
7. No social cut runs over 2:20, the X limit; Bluesky and Shorts allow 3:00.
8. The master is 16:9 at 1920 x 1080; feed cuts are 4:5 or 1:1 (LinkedIn, Facebook, Buffer).
9. Every feature reads with sound off: a headline names it and the action shows it (Facebook, Notion).
10. A headline has at most 6 words and 42 characters on one line (Netflix).
11. Text stays up for (words / 2.67) s plus 1 s, and at least 2 s; 6 words get 3.3 s (Ofcom 160 wpm; the 1 s margin for non-native readers is my choice).
12. Text and captions have at least 4.5:1 contrast against what is behind them (WCAG 1.4.3 by analogy).
13. Nothing flashes more than 3 times in 1 s, including fast dark and light theme switches (WCAG 2.3.1).
14. Captions ship as a separate SRT or VTT file even when text is burned in, so viewers can translate them (Rev, LinkedIn, Bluesky).
15. Zoom is 1.5x to 2.0x and never above 2.5x (Cap default 2.0x; the 2.5x cap is my judgment against WebKit's zoom trigger).
16. A zoom starts 0.3 s before the click and holds 2.5 s after it; zooms less than 2.5 s apart merge (Cap).
17. Zooms use a near-critically damped spring: 90 % in about 0.3 s, settled by 0.5 s, no overshoot (Cap, Screen Studio "Focused").
18. No rotation, parallax, 3D tilt or fast side pans; motion blur stays light or off (WebKit, Apple).
19. The cursor is enlarged, spring-smoothed and hidden when idle; a click ripple lasts about 0.6 s (Cap, Screen Studio).
20. Keystroke labels show shortcuts only, never typed text (Screen Studio).
21. Capture and export at 60 fps; only paid LinkedIn ads get a 30 fps copy (Screen Studio, KDE, LinkedIn ads).
22. Record at device scale factor 2, so 13 px UI text is 26 px in the frame and at least 50 px when zoomed (my arithmetic).
23. The window sits on a plain background with even padding, corners of about 30 px at 1080p and a soft shadow (Cap).
24. Music is a YouTube Audio Library track with "Attribution not required", or CC0 or CC BY with credit in the description; never NoDerivatives (YouTube Help, CC BY 4.0).
25. The end card shows the upgrade command, the release notes link and a thanks to the contributors with their count (JupyterLab 4.0 post, Blender, GNOME).

Open questions: synthetic voices, loudness target, use of Audio Library music off YouTube, Pixabay Content ID claims, Tella, FocuSee and Rotato defaults, and who makes the GNOME films.
